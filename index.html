Fentanyl administration Unit

Atlas MacDonald's Atlas

The AAP endorses premedication for nonemergent intubation, but there's no standardized regimen. A typical practice is atropine, fentanyl, and a nondepolarizing paralytic. Confirm doses with your unit's order set.

Needle decompression

Emergency only, for a critically compromised baby. It buys time while you set up the chest tube.

Site Atlas

2nd intercostal space, midclavicular line, or 4th intercostal space, anterior axillary line. Avoid the breast bud. The Atlas prefers the anterior approach so the lateral chest stays clear for the tube.

Equipment Atlas

20- or 22-gauge angiocath, T-connector, 3-way stopcock, 10 and 20 mL syringes.

Differs: the Atlas says a catheter-over-needle is less traumatic than straight needles or butterfly (scalp vein) sets. Other guidelines use a 23–25G butterfly (Safer Care Victoria) or 18–24G angiocath (Cochrane review).

Technique Atlas

  1. Pull the skin sideways to make a Z-track.
  2. Insert perpendicular, just over the top of the lower rib.
  3. Advance slowly until you lose resistance, see humidity, or hear air. Slide the catheter in and remove the needle.
  4. Attach the T-connector and stopcock and evacuate with the syringe rather than leaving it open to air.

If a tube will follow, remove air only until vital signs stabilize. The leftover air pocket makes tube insertion safer.

For a nontension pneumothorax, especially in a baby not on positive pressure, needle evacuation is sometimes all that's needed.

Sources: Atlas ch. 41, Thoracostomy; Safer Care Victoria, Pneumothorax drainage for neonates (2014); Cochrane review CD011724 (2019).

Chest tube

Site Atlas

4th (or 5th) intercostal space, just anterior to the midaxillary line. A horizontal line from the nipple marks about the 4th space. Enter over the top of the rib.

Size

Pigtail Atlas6 or 8 Fr if <28 wk
8 Fr if >28 wk
Straight tube Atlas8–12 Fr

Differs: Safer Care Victoria uses a pigtail of 8 Fr under 1500 g and 10 Fr over 1500 g.

Technique Atlas

Pigtails go in percutaneously. For a straight tube, a trocar is not recommended because of the risk of lung perforation; use blunt dissection and puncture the pleura with closed forceps.

For air, direct the tip cephalad and anterior, toward the apex (midclavicle), with all side holes inside the chest.

Suction Atlas

10–15 cm H₂O for most ventilated infants. Too much suction can pull tissue into the side holes.

Confirm Atlas

AP and lateral films, both, to confirm the anterior course and that the side holes are in the chest. Bubbling pattern and ultrasound help.

Sources: Atlas ch. 41, Thoracostomy; Safer Care Victoria, Pneumothorax drainage for neonates (2014).

Lumbar puncture

Level Atlas

Use L3–L4 or L4–L5. Use the lower space for <28 wk.

The line between the iliac crests is usually said to cross L4 or L4–L5, but in neonates it can cross as low as L5. Palpate carefully.

Position Atlas

Sitting with hips flexed gives the widest spaces; lateral decubitus with hips flexed is second. Don't flex the neck: it doesn't widen the spaces and raises the risk of airway obstruction. Lying on the side with knees flexed can cause brief desaturation, so watch the monitor.

Needle Atlas

Bevelled spinal needle with stylet: 25G × 1 inch or 22G × 1.5 inch. Always have the stylet in to pass the skin (epidermoid tumor risk).

Midline, angled slightly cephalad toward the umbilicus. Once past the skin, either take the stylet out and advance slowly, or leave it in and check often. Stylet-out has a higher first-try success, but whether it raises the epidermoid risk is unclear. Replace the stylet before removing the needle.

Don't do it if Atlas

Signs of raised intracranial pressure (image first), uncorrected thrombocytopenia or bleeding disorder, skin infection at the site, lumbosacral anomalies, or instability where the risk outweighs the benefit.

Exchange transfusion

Blood volume Atlas

Term about 80–85 mL/kg. Preterm about 100–120 mL/kg.

Other guidelines use term 80–90 and preterm 90–100 mL/kg.

How much Atlas

Double volume2 × blood volume. Exchanges about 85% of the blood volume.
Single volume1 × blood volume. Exchanges about 60%.
Partial, severe anemiaBlood volume × (Hb desired − Hb initial) ÷ (Hb of PRBC − Hb initial)
Partial, polycythemiaBlood volume × desired Hct change ÷ initial Hct

Order an extra 25–30 mL for the tubing and blood warmer.

Rate Atlas

5 mL/kg per 2–4 minute cycle. Draw and infuse slowly, at least a minute each, to avoid swings in blood pressure and intracranial pressure.

Method Atlas

Push-pull: through a UVC; single lumen preferred, since double lumen adds resistance.
Isovolumetric: donor blood in through the UVC or a PIV, baby's blood out through a UAC, UVC, or peripheral arterial line. Better tolerated in sick or unstable babies.

Blood Atlas

As fresh as possible (<7 days), irradiated within 24 hours of use, through a thermostatic blood warmer. Hct 40–45% is preferred for hyperbilirubinemia.

Before, during, after Atlas

Before: draw any diagnostic tests first (serologies, metabolic screen, genetics), plus Hb/Hct, platelets, electrolytes, calcium, gas, glucose, total and direct bilirubin, and coags. NPO 4 hours if possible; empty the stomach; start a PIV for glucose.

During: glucose every 60 minutes; gases as needed.

After: glucose every 2–4 hours for 24 hours. Ionized calcium and platelets right away in sick or preterm babies. Hb, Hct, and bilirubin about 4 hours later.

Sources: Atlas ch. 49, Exchange Transfusions; Brigham and Women's Newborn Medicine guideline; Italian recommendations for transfusion therapy in neonatology (PMC4607607).

Peripheral arterial line

Sites Atlas

Radial is most common, then ulnar and posterior tibial; dorsalis pedis occasionally. Avoid axillary and brachial (poor collateral flow) and temporal (neurologic risk). Use the right arm when you need preductal values.

One NICU guideline (Leeds) calls the umbilical arterial line the arterial line of choice in the newborn.

Collateral check Atlas

Do an Allen test before a radial line, but it's not fully reliable, so keep watching perfusion during and after. For the foot, occlude dorsalis pedis and posterior tibial, release one, and look for reperfusion within 10 seconds. Don't hyperextend the wrist; it can occlude the artery and give a false result.

Catheter Atlas

24G (0.56 or 0.75 inch) or 22G × 1 inch. Transilluminate the wrist in 45–60° extension. Flush gently with 0.5 mL and watch for blanching.

Infusion Atlas Unit

0.5–0.95 NS with heparin 1–2 units/mL at 1 mL/hr, started right away. Below 1 mL/hr, blood tends to back up and clot.

Other guidelines: 1 unit/mL at 0.5–1 mL/hr; 0.5 units/mL for preterm; 2 units/mL at 0.3–0.5 mL/hr.

Use it for sampling and pressure only; nothing but the heparinized flush runs through it.

Don't place if Atlas

Uncorrectable bleeding disorder, poor perfusion or collateral flow in that limb, local infection, limb malformation, or previous surgery there.

Sources: Atlas ch. 33, Peripheral Arterial Cannulation; Leeds Teaching Hospitals neonatal PAL guideline; Safer Care Victoria; CAHS WA; Ashford and St Peter's.
